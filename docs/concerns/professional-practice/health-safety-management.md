# <i class="fa-solid fa-helmet-safety" style="font-size:20px"></i> Health & Safety Management

[TOC]


Systematic identification, assessment, and control of occupational health and safety risks.

## Health and Safety Context

Safety maturity from hazard identification to exemplary safety culture in horticulture.

1. Horticultural work presents a distinctive risk profile combining outdoor exposure, manual handling of heavy materials, powered machinery operation, COSHH-regulated chemical handling, and working at height in arboricultural operations.
2. Establish comprehensive safety management from hazard identification through controls implementation to proactive safety culture, meeting statutory requirements and exceeding sector benchmarks.
3. Implement structured risk assessment programmes, COSHH assessments for all substances, safe systems of work, health surveillance, and behavioural safety observations with leading indicator tracking.
4. Sustained zero serious harm record, ISO 45001 accreditation, and safety practices adopted by the sector as benchmark standards.

## States

### 1. Identified

Hazards and legal obligations formally recognized.

**Checklist:**

- [ ] **Hazard Register Compiled**: A register of all hazards including manual handling (heavy materials, repetitive tasks), machinery operation (mowers, strimmers, chainsaws), chemical exposure (pesticides, fertilizers under COSHH), and environmental factors (weather extremes, outdoor exposure, lone working) is demonstrably compiled using sector-specific risk profiles.
- [ ] **Statutory Obligations Identified**: Statutory obligations under the Health and Safety at Work Act 1974, Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999, Personal Protective Equipment Regulations 1992, and Provision and Use of Work Equipment Regulations 1998 are demonstrably identified with documented register.
- [ ] **COSHH Substances Listed**: COSHH-regulated substances are demonstrably listed with safety data sheets obtained from suppliers and accessible to all personnel using the products, stored in bunded cabinets meeting regulatory requirements.
- [ ] **High-Risk Activities Categorized**: High-risk activities including chainsaw operation (NPTC certification required), working at height (tree pruning, hedge trimming above 2 meters), pesticide application (PA1/PA6 mandatory), and vehicle operation are demonstrably identified and categorized by risk level.
- [ ] **Lone Working Risks Documented**: Lone working situations (estate management, remote site operations, winter maintenance) and their associated risks (medical emergencies without assistance, violence from public, environmental hazards) are demonstrably recognized and documented with control requirements specified.

### 2. Controlled

Risk controls systematically implemented following hierarchy.

**Checklist:**

- [ ] **Risk Assessments Completed**: Written risk assessments following the hierarchy of control (elimination, substitution, engineering controls, administrative controls, PPE) are demonstrably completed for all identified hazards with documented control measures and responsible persons assigned.
- [ ] **COSHH Assessments Current**: COSHH assessments for all chemical products (pesticides, fertilizers, cleaning agents, fuels) are demonstrably current with exposure limits documented, control measures specified (ventilation, PPE, safe handling procedures), and review dates within 12-month intervals.
- [ ] **PPE Provision Operational**: PPE provision (safety boots, gloves, eye protection, hearing protection, high-visibility clothing), training in correct usage, fit testing (respiratory protection), and replacement procedures (damaged equipment disposal) are demonstrably operational with issue records maintained.
- [ ] **Safe Work Systems Documented**: Safe systems of work for high-risk activities including chainsaw use (NPTC CS30/31 standards), pesticide application (mixing procedures, application techniques, re-entry intervals), working at height (ladder safety, mobile elevated work platforms), and machinery operation are demonstrably documented.
- [ ] **Emergency Procedures Established**: First aid provision (qualified first aiders, first aid kits on vehicles and at base), emergency procedures (severe injury response, chemical spillage protocols), and RIDDOR-compliant accident reporting (HSE notification within statutory timescales) are demonstrably in place.
- [ ] **Machinery Safety Enforced**: Machinery safety procedures including pre-use checks (daily inspection sheets), LOLER examinations for lifting equipment (annual thorough examination), operator competency verification (NPTC certificates, training records), and guarding maintenance are demonstrably enforced.

### 3. Managed

Safety performance monitored through data analysis.

**Checklist:**

- [ ] **Accident Data Analyzed**: Accident and incident data including near-miss reporting (hazardous occurrences without injury), lost-time injuries (days absent from work), and property damage events is demonstrably analysed quarterly for trends, root causes, and preventive actions with management review documentation.
- [ ] **Health Surveillance Conducted**: Health surveillance for workers exposed to vibration (Hand-Arm Vibration Syndrome monitoring with tool usage logs), noise (audiometry testing for those exceeding 85dB exposure), or sensitizing substances (skin checks for pesticide handlers, respiratory assessments) is demonstrably conducted with medical records confidentially maintained.
- [ ] **Safety Inspections Systematic**: Safety inspections of work sites (hazard walkthroughs, client site safety assessments), equipment (weekly machinery checks, monthly PPE inspections), and facilities (storage areas, workshops, welfare facilities) are demonstrably carried out at defined frequencies with corrective action tracking.
- [ ] **Toolbox Talks Delivered**: Toolbox talks and safety briefings are demonstrably delivered before commencing work on new sites covering site-specific hazards (public access, underground services, overhead cables, water features), task-specific risks (tree work near buildings, pesticide use restrictions), and emergency procedures.
- [ ] **Safety Training Current**: Safety training records demonstrably evidence current competencies for all personnel with NPTC certificates (chainsaw, pesticide application, machinery operation), first aid qualifications (Emergency First Aid at Work minimum, First Aid at Work for supervisors), and refresher training within validity periods.

### 4. Proactive

Safety culture prevents incidents before occurrence.

**Checklist:**

- [ ] **Leading Indicators Tracked**: Leading safety indicators including training completion rates (percentage achieving NPTC certifications on schedule), near-miss reporting frequency (target 10:1 near-miss to injury ratio), safety observation participation (employee-led hazard spotting), and proactive risk assessment updates are demonstrably tracked monthly.
- [ ] **Behavioral Observations Active**: Behavioural safety observations identifying at-risk practices (shortcuts bypassing safe systems, PPE non-compliance, fatigue-related errors) are demonstrably conducted by supervisors and peers with non-punitive feedback conversations and systemic issue escalation to management.
- [ ] **Worker Safety Engagement Active**: Worker engagement in safety improvement through consultation (safety committee meetings with employee representation, toolbox talk participation, hazard reporting schemes) or formal structures (elected safety representatives, union safety partnerships) is demonstrably active with documented influence on safety decisions.
- [ ] **Mental Health Support Provided**: Mental health and wellbeing support addressing physically demanding horticultural work (musculoskeletal strain, fatigue management), seasonal employment stress (winter income uncertainty, job security), and lone working isolation is demonstrably provided through occupational health access, manager training (Mental Health First Aid), and confidential support services.
- [ ] **Safety Performance Exceeding Benchmarks**: Safety performance demonstrably exceeds sector benchmarks with continuous improvement trends shown through injury frequency rates below industry averages (HSE Agriculture, Forestry & Fishing statistics), zero RIDDOR-reportable incidents over sustained periods (12+ months), and improving leading indicator scores.

### 5. Exemplary

Safety practices set industry standards and benchmarks.

**Checklist:**

- [ ] **Safety System Accreditation Achieved**: Safety management system accreditation such as ISO 45001 (Occupational Health and Safety Management Systems) is demonstrably achieved with third-party certification audit compliance, management review processes operational, and continuous improvement cycles documented.
- [ ] **Sector Guidance Contribution Evidenced**: Contribution to sector safety guidance through industry body participation (BALI Safety Committee membership, HTA working groups), published safety case studies (sharing learnings from incident investigations), or training provider partnerships (guest lecturing on horticultural safety) is demonstrably evidenced.
- [ ] **Zero Serious Harm Sustained**: Zero serious harm track record (no fatalities, no permanent disabilities, no major injuries requiring hospitalization over 24 hours) maintained over a sustained period (3+ years) is demonstrably documented with annual safety performance reporting and external verification.
- [ ] **Safety Innovation Implemented**: Safety innovation including novel equipment reducing risk (ergonomic tool designs, automation of high-risk tasks), procedures preventing common injuries (mandatory work rotation preventing repetitive strain, heat stress protocols), or technology adoption (wearable safety monitors, proximity detection systems) is demonstrably implemented.
- [ ] **Safety Practices Benchmarked**: The organization's safety practices are demonstrably referenced by industry bodies as benchmarks through published case studies in BALI/HTA resources, recognition in HSE sector guidance, safety award achievements, or adoption of practices by peer organizations following site visits or knowledge sharing.
