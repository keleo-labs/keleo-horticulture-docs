# <i class="fa-solid fa-graduation-cap" style="font-size:20px"></i> Workforce Development

[TOC]


Structured development of horticultural workforce capability through qualifications and career pathways.

## Workforce Development Context

Structured workforce development from skills audit to sector-leading practice.

1. The UK horticultural workforce has an average decision-maker age of 55, predominantly informal skill acquisition, and a 3% supervisory ratio that constrains operational growth.
2. Build structured development pathways from Level 2 apprenticeship through RHS qualifications to Chartered professional status, while addressing the supervisory capacity gap.
3. Implement individual development plans, apprenticeship programmes aligned with Skills England standards, CPD tracking against CIH requirements, and supervisory training initiatives.
4. Measurable improvement in qualification attainment rates, internal promotion, employee retention, and organizational capability, with the enterprise contributing to sector workforce standards.

## States

### 1. Identified

Workforce skill requirements formally mapped.

**Checklist:**

- [ ] **Competency Audit Completed**: Current workforce competencies and qualification levels are demonstrably documented for all roles using competency matrices mapping individuals against RHS Level 2/3 standards, City & Guilds NPTC certificates, and professional memberships.
- [ ] **Skills Gap Analysis Conducted**: Skill gaps against business requirements and AHDB Ornamental Horticulture Skills Survey 2019 benchmarks (technical knowledge deficits, supervisory capability shortfalls, digital technology adoption needs) are demonstrably identified with prioritized action plans.
- [ ] **Recruitment Challenges Analyzed**: Recruitment challenges including hard-to-fill roles (qualified supervisors, specialist arborists, plant health experts), seasonal labour dependencies (peak spring workforce expansion), and retention barriers (pay competitiveness, career progression limitations) are demonstrably analysed.
- [ ] **Career Pathways Mapped**: Qualification pathways from entry-level (Level 2 apprenticeships, RHS Level 2 Certificate) through intermediate (RHS Level 3 Diploma, NPTC specialist certifications) to professional status (CIH Chartered Horticulturist, Landscape Institute pathways) are demonstrably understood and documented.
- [ ] **Demographic Succession Risk Assessed**: The impact of workforce demographics including age profile (average decision-maker age of 55 years identified by Cardiff University Knowing to Grow research), impending retirements, and succession risks for specialized knowledge holders is demonstrably assessed.

### 2. Structured

Development programmes formally established and resourced.

**Checklist:**

- [ ] **Apprenticeship Programmes Established**: Apprenticeship programmes aligned with Skills England standards ST0225 (Horticulture or Landscape Operative Level 2) and ST0226 (Horticulture or Landscape Technician Level 3) are demonstrably offered or planned with training provider partnerships and levy funding utilization.
- [ ] **Individual Development Plans Active**: Individual development plans linking employees to RHS qualification pathways (Levels 2-5), City and Guilds NPTC certificates (machinery, pesticides), or degree programmes are demonstrably in place with annual review cycles and employer-supported study time.
- [ ] **Supervisory Development Programmes Running**: Supervisory development addressing the industry 3% supervisor gap through structured leadership training (ILM Level 3 management qualifications, team leadership courses, mentoring by senior managers) is demonstrably operational with progression from operative to supervisor tracked.
- [ ] **Induction Programmes Standardized**: Induction programmes for new starters covering safety (PPE requirements, emergency procedures, hazard reporting), compliance (COSHH protocols, pesticide regulations, waste management), and technical fundamentals (plant identification, tool use, quality standards) are demonstrably standardized.
- [ ] **Mentoring Systems Functioning**: Mentoring arrangements pairing experienced practitioners (10+ years industry experience, Level 3+ qualifications) with trainees (apprentices, recent hires, career changers) are demonstrably functioning with structured mentoring plans and regular progress reviews.

### 3. Progressive

Workforce capability measurably advancing.

**Checklist:**

- [ ] **Qualification Attainment Trending Upward**: Qualification attainment rates across the workforce (percentage holding Level 2+, Level 3+, professional memberships) are demonstrably tracked annually and trending upward with year-on-year improvements demonstrating investment effectiveness.
- [ ] **CPD Participation Meeting Standards**: CPD participation meeting CIH benchmarks of 30 hours annually for professional staff and RHS continuing education requirements is demonstrably evidenced through CPD records, training attendance logs, and professional body reporting.
- [ ] **Internal Promotion Demonstrated**: Internal promotion from operative to supervisory and management roles is demonstrably occurring with documented progression examples showing career advancement enabled by development programmes (apprentice to supervisor, technician to manager).
- [ ] **Workforce Diversity Improving**: Workforce diversity across age (under-30 representation increasing), gender (women in technical and supervisory roles growing), and background (career changers, graduates, international recruits) is demonstrably improving against sector norms identified by AHDB surveys.
- [ ] **Training Investment Benchmarked**: Training investment as a proportion of revenue (industry benchmark 2-3% for progressive firms) is demonstrably measured annually with ROI tracking linking training expenditure to productivity improvements, quality enhancements, or reduced recruitment costs.
- [ ] **Retention Rates Improving**: Employee retention rates are demonstrably improved through development programme participation with lower turnover among staff enrolled in apprenticeships, qualification programmes, or career development plans compared to baseline workforce turnover.

### 4. Embedded

Learning culture integrated into daily operations.

**Checklist:**

- [ ] **Knowledge Sharing Routine**: Knowledge sharing through structured peer learning (monthly technical sessions led by experienced staff), toolbox talks (weekly on-site briefings covering techniques, safety, new practices), and technical demonstrations (seasonal pruning workshops, IPM training) is demonstrably routine.
- [ ] **External Learning Hosting Active**: External learning contributions through hosting apprentices from training providers (Pershore College, Writtle University College, Myerscough College), student placements (undergraduate work experience, school engagement), or industry visits (garden club tours, professional delegations) are demonstrably made.
- [ ] **Cross-Functional Skills Developed**: Cross-functional skill development enabling workforce flexibility (horticulture staff gaining machinery operation competence, maintenance team developing plant knowledge, supervisors acquiring business administration skills) is demonstrably achieved through job rotation and multi-skilling programmes.
- [ ] **Workplace Assessment Integrated**: Continuous workplace assessment aligned with 2025-2026 Skills England reforms emphasizing on-the-job evaluation rather than solely off-site examinations is demonstrably adopted with assessors observing competence during normal work activities.
- [ ] **Training Provider Recognition Achieved**: The organization is demonstrably recognized as a quality training provider by awarding bodies (RHS Approved Centre status), training providers (preferred placement employer), or colleges (industry partner contributing to curriculum development).

### 5. Exemplary

Organization shapes sector workforce development standards.

**Checklist:**

- [ ] **Standards Development Contribution Evidenced**: Contribution to developing apprenticeship standards (Skills England trailblazer group participation), professional competency models (CIH competency framework development), or qualification design (RHS qualification advisory panels) is demonstrably evidenced through documented involvement.
- [ ] **Workforce Case Studies Published**: Published workforce development case studies advancing sector practice are demonstrably attributed to the organization through AHDB knowledge exchange publications, Landscape Institute professional journals, or CIH best practice resources.
- [ ] **Benchmark-Exceeding Outcomes Achieved**: Workforce outcomes demonstrably exceed sector benchmarks across qualification attainment (80%+ staff holding Level 3+ vs. sector average 45%), retention (annual turnover below 15% vs. sector 25%+), and progression (30%+ internal promotion rate).
- [ ] **Curriculum Partnerships Active**: Partnership with educational institutions in curriculum development (industry advisory board membership at Writtle, Pershore, Myerscough), assessment innovation (developing workplace assessment protocols), or facilities sharing (hosting student field trips, providing guest lecturers) is demonstrably active.
- [ ] **Workforce Model Referenced as Exemplary**: The organization's workforce model is demonstrably referenced by industry bodies as exemplary practice through citations in AHDB guidance, inclusion in BALI best practice resources, or presentation at HTA/RHS conferences as workforce development case study.
